# Brief: 3D Explorer — the map comes alive

**For:** an AI coding agent (Fable) working autonomously in this repo.
**From:** the founder (product) and the previous engineering session.
**Status:** Phases 0-2 implemented; Phase 3 is next. Review the viewer on a real phone before
proceeding. Stop at every human checkpoint marked 🧑.

---

## 1. What we want, in the founder's words

> "We start with simulators, not the camera. Just tapping on the web app or phone app pops up the
> mountains and shows full HD 3D with all areas. Hitting *Show river* shows the 3D river flowing all
> the way from one point to the sea. Like Unreal Engine games."

What this means:

- **Tap Mount Kenya on the 2D map.** The map tilts into 3D, the land rises out of the flat paper (the "pop-up"), and the camera settles on a detailed, lit, shaded Mount Kenya. The child can orbit, zoom and tap other places.
- **Tap _Show river_ on the River Tana.** The camera finds the source in the highlands, then water visibly flows along the real course of the river, downhill, about 1,000 km to the Indian Ocean. The camera follows it.
- **The same for every feature kind:** lakes fill to their real surface level, the Rift Valley shows its escarpments, the coast shows the drop into the ocean.
- **No camera, no AR.** This is a simulator. It is reached from the existing Tier C web app, which already runs on phones. AR comes later and will reuse your assets.

### Be honest about "Unreal-like"

Our learners use budget Android phones (2–3 GB RAM, Mali/Adreno GPUs) on paid mobile data, in a browser. Real-time Unreal 5 tricks (Nanite, Lumen) are not possible there. The target is **"documentary-map realism"**: what you would see in a nature-documentary flyover or a premium globe app. We get there by **baking**: compute lighting, ambient occlusion, detail normals and land-cover colour offline in the generator, so the phone only draws a modest mesh with rich textures. Real-time effects are added only on devices that can afford them.

If the target is too ambitious for a budget, say so with measurements. Do not quietly ship something slow.

## 3. Non-negotiables for this work

1. **No branching on pack, feature or country ids.** Behaviour comes from `feature.kind` plus data. Mountain, lake, river, valley, coast, ocean, city and plateau each get a *scene behaviour*. Grade 6 East Africa must get 3D by adding a pack and running the generator, with no code change. Prove this with a feature test, as `tests/features/api/new-grade-is-a-content-drop.feature.test.ts` does.
2. **Facts shown to children come from the pack, never from the DEM.** The DEM gives geometry only. A height label for Mount Kenya shows the teacher-approved fact ("about 5,199 m"), not a raw DEM sample such as 5,163 m. Two different numbers on screen would confuse a child.
3. **Vertical exaggeration is shown, not hidden.** Kenya at true scale looks flat, so we exaggerate heights: about ×3 in the country view and about ×1.5 close-up. When it is on, show a small "Heights ×3" note. 🧑 The founder and a teacher confirm the final wording.
4. **No third-party map services at runtime.** No Mapbox, Google, Cesium ion or similar. Every tile, texture and font is generated by us and served from our own origin. This is for privacy, cost and offline use. An E2E test asserts that every request is same-origin.
5. **A mission never waits on 3D.** 3D is an enhancement. With no WebGL2, a low-memory device, or assets still downloading, the 2D map keeps working. Missions must not depend on 3D.
6. **Data budget for Kenyan mobile data**, enforced by an automated test:
   - The initial web app load does not grow. Today it is about 145 KB gzip of JS, and the 3D code is a lazy chunk.
   - Opening 3D for the first time costs ≤ 3 MB.
   - Each HD feature patch is ≤ 1.5 MB.
   - All 3D assets for one pack total ≤ 25 MB.
   - Assets are cached after the first view.
7. **Licences are verified and recorded before data enters the repo** (see §5). 🧑 Stop and ask if a licence is unclear or non-commercial.
8. **Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.** Use cuts instead of fly-throughs, and a static river with an animated arrow instead of the flow sweep.
9. **Map content goes through teacher review.** Borders are sensitive. The Ilemi Triangle (Kenya, South Sudan and Ethiopia) is disputed. Use the same border source as the 2D basemap (Natural Earth through `world-atlas`) and flag the depiction for teacher review in the asset manifest.

## 4. Architecture decisions (already made; write them up as ADRs)

Write these as `docs/adr/0008-3d-rendering-stack.md` and `docs/adr/0009-geodata-sources-and-3d-assets.md` in Phase 0. If you find a decision is wrong, argue it in the ADR with evidence and change it. Do not drift silently.

### 4.1 Rendering: three.js in the web app, with assets Unity can also use

- **Web:** `three` + `@react-three/fiber` (v9 supports React 19) + `@react-three/drei`. Pin exact versions that are at least two weeks old, and check their peer dependencies.
- **Rejected alternatives, with reasons, for the ADR:**
  - *Unreal Engine.* No web export. Pixel Streaming needs cloud GPUs per user (cost) and breaks offline use. Very large APKs.
  - *Unity WebGL build.* Tens of MB before anything appears, which breaks the data budget.
  - *CesiumJS.* A globe engine, heavy, and its best data lives on Cesium ion (a third-party runtime service).
  - *Babylon.js.* Viable. three.js wins on the React integration and its ecosystem. Note it as a fallback.
- **Unity (phone app, ADR-0002):** the generator also writes Unity-ready outputs (16-bit RAW heightmaps, the same textures, river JSON). Unity is **not installed** on the dev machine, so Unity scene work is out of scope (Phase 5). The web app already runs on phones, so the founder's demo needs no Unity.

### 4.2 Geodata pipeline: a generator tool, assets committed with a manifest

- **New workspace `tools/geo`.** It is a Node/TypeScript CLI. It does I/O, so it must **not** live in `packages/*`, which stay pure. Add it to the root `workspaces` list.
- **Inputs:** every pack in `packages/content/packs/*.json`, read from `printTarget.bounds` and `features`. The generator never mentions a pack id.
- **Download cache:** raw downloads go to `.cache/geo/` (gitignored).
- **Outputs** go to `content-assets/<packId>/3d/` and are committed with **Git LFS**. Extend `.gitattributes`, which is currently scoped to `apps/unity/**`. The outputs include a `manifest.json` that records:
  - the source datasets with version and licence,
  - parameters,
  - a SHA-256 for every file,
  - the review flags.
- **Why commit the outputs rather than generate them in CI:** teachers approve the *exact* assets that ship, builds work offline and reproducibly, and CI never downloads gigabytes. CI checks the manifest instead (hashes match, budgets hold). `npm run geo:build` regenerates the outputs.
- **Pipeline stages:**
  1. **Elevation.**
     - Use Copernicus DEM. Its COG files are on the AWS Open Data registry, public HTTPS, no auth.
     - Use **GLO-90** (or the COG's internal overviews) for the country and regional levels, and **GLO-30** only inside HD feature patches.
     - Do **not** download every 1° tile at 30 m: the Kenya bounds cover about 99 tiles, several GB.
     - Read COGs with range requests (the `geotiff` npm package).
     - Tiles over the ocean are missing; treat them as sea or bathymetry.
     - Optional: add GEBCO bathymetry for the Indian Ocean shelf, which makes a striking coast.
  2. **Tiles.** Standard XYZ Web Mercator tiles. Mercator distortion is negligible at Kenya's latitude, and a standard layout lets you check tiles with ordinary tools.
     - Encode heights as **Terrain-RGB** in lossless WebP: `h = -10000 + (R·65536 + G·256 + B) × 0.1`.
     - Three levels of detail:
       - **L0 country overview:** one mesh for the whole pack, about 1 km per pixel.
       - **L1 regional tiles:** about 150 m per pixel, streamed around the camera.
       - **L2 HD feature patches:** about 30–40 m per pixel, only around features that need close-ups (mountains, valley segments, lake shores). The patch extent depends on the feature kind, for example 40 × 40 km for a mountain.
  3. **Meshing.** Build meshes in the browser with RTIN (`@mapbox/martini`): an adaptive triangle count with an error threshold set per quality tier. Add skirts so cracks between tiles are hidden.
  4. **Baked textures** at a higher resolution than the mesh. This is the main "HD on a cheap phone" trick.
     - **Albedo:** hypsometric tint blended with land cover from **ESA WorldCover 10 m**, so forest shows on Mount Kenya's slopes, dryland in the north, and farmland in the highlands. Add water and a faint summit snow tint.
     - **Baked relief light:** sun shading and darkened hollows in one greyscale image, from the finest elevation data used for that level. *(Phase 1: this replaces a separate normal map, which lossy WebP damages and lossless WebP makes too big; see ADR-0009.)* Real-time shadows only on the high tier.
  5. **Rivers** (decided in [ADR-0009](../adr/0009-geodata-sources-and-3d-assets.md) section 3; approved 2026-11-16).
     - We trace rivers ourselves on the Copernicus elevation data. No licensed river network is used.
     - Start from the pack's rough `path`; its first point is the source end. The river starts on the
       biggest stream within a few km of that point.
     - Flood the land upward from the sea (or from the lake under the line's last point) and remember,
       for every cell, the way down. Following that from the start gives the real course **all the way
       to the sea**, climbing out of hollows the way water fills and spills them.
     - Natural Earth's public-domain river lines are the guide: only land in a corridor around them can
       carry the river, and in flat land the trace is pulled gently toward them. Gaps in the surveyed
       line (it is drawn in pieces, broken at reservoirs) are bridged.
     - OpenStreetMap is the pre-approved fallback if a river comes out visibly wrong; switching needs
       a note in ADR-0009 and the ODbL attribution.
     - Output: a line ordered from source to mouth with distance along the river, elevation smoothed
       to be **non-increasing** (water never flows uphill on screen), and a width hint from the
       upstream area (the river widens downstream).
     - Carve a shallow channel into the terrain along the river.
  6. **Lakes:** a flat water surface at the lake's surface level, with a shoreline from the water mask.

### 4.3 Viewer behaviour depends on feature kind

`apps/web/src/three/` (a lazy chunk) holds a pure, unit-testable **scene planner**. It maps `(feature, manifest, quality tier)` to:
- camera start and end poses,
- which LODs to load,
- labels,
- the animation script.

Rendering only executes that plan. Examples:

| Kind | Scene |
|---|---|
| mountain | Land rises from flat (exaggeration animates from 0 to target, the "pop-up"), then the camera orbits the summit. The height label shows the **pack fact**. |
| river | **Show river.** The camera goes to the source. A water front sweeps downstream (a shader `progress` uniform from 0 to 1 along the river's distance), with a flow texture scrolling in the flow direction. The camera follows on a smoothed spline, and the scene ends at the mouth with the ocean in view. |
| lake | The water surface fills up to its level. The camera circles the shore. |
| valley | A fly-through along `path` with the escarpments lit from the side. |
| coast / ocean | The land falls to the shelf and the deep ocean (if bathymetry is present). |
| city | A pin and its regional context. No 3D buildings. |

**Labels:** HTML labels in **en/sw** (the current app locale), hidden when terrain blocks them, with large touch targets. Tapping a label in 3D opens that feature's fact card, the same as in 2D.

**Quality tiers:** `low`, `medium` and `high`, chosen from `navigator.deviceMemory`, WebGL2 capabilities, and a 2-second frame-time probe that steps down automatically. Show the tier in a debug overlay (`?debug=3d`). The `low` tier must hold **≥ 30 fps on a 2–3 GB Android phone**. 🧑 The founder measures this on a real phone with `npm run dev:phone`.

**Offline:** cache-first caching of `content-assets/**` (a small service worker, or `vite-plugin-pwa` if justified in the ADR). Once a feature has been viewed, it works in airplane mode.

| Data | Use | Licence to **verify** |
|---|---|---|
| Copernicus DEM GLO-30 / GLO-90 | Elevation | Expected: free incl. commercial, attribution required |

## 6. Phases, acceptance criteria and checkpoints

Work on a new branch **`feat/3d-explorer`** cut from the current `feat/dunialab-foundation` HEAD. PR #1 is under review; do not push to it. Commit with `--no-gpg-sign`, one or more commits per phase, and say in each commit message which phase it belongs to.

**Every phase follows our TDD rule:**
1. Write the feature test(s) first and run them red.
2. Write unit tests and implement until they pass.
3. Add or extend an E2E test.
4. Update the architecture docs in the same change: the Mermaid components diagram, a new flow in `flows.md`, a change-log line, ADRs where trade-offs were made. Also update `technical-guide.md` and the repo map and commands in `CLAUDE.md`.

**Feature tests must not hit the network.**
- For pipeline *logic*, use small fixtures: a synthetic DEM, and a tiny river network with known `NEXT_DOWN` links and a deliberate cycle.
- For *real-world* checks ("Mount Kenya is about 5,200 m"), test the **committed generated assets**. They are what ships.

### Phase 0: decisions and a de-risking spike (small)
- **ADRs:** write ADR-0008 and ADR-0009 and verify every licence in §5.
- **Spike:** prove headless Chromium in our E2E setup can render WebGL2 (SwiftShader), and that a canvas pixel read-back works. All later E2E tests depend on this.
- **Spike:** read a single Copernicus COG tile window with range requests from Node on Windows. Record the bytes downloaded.
- **Budgets:** write down every budget from §3.6 as constants that tests import.
- 🧑 **Checkpoint:** the founder reads both ADRs, including the licence table, before any data is committed.

### Phase 1: the geodata generator (`tools/geo`)
Feature tests (examples; write them in our Given/When/Then style):
- **Mountains are real.** In the committed L2 patch around the `mount-kenya` anchor, the highest point within 15 km is between 4,900 and 5,300 m. Around `kilimanjaro` it is between 5,600 and 5,950 m.
- **The Tana reaches the sea.**
  - The traced river starts within 10 km of the first point of the pack's `path` and ends at the ocean (elevation ≤ 5 m, next to sea pixels).
  - Its length is believable. *(Phase 1 measured this from the pack's starting point; see
    ADR-0009 "What Phase 1 built". The original 850 to 1,150 km band assumed the river's farthest source.)*
  - Its elevation never increases from source to mouth.
- **Lakes are flat.** The Lake Victoria surface sits at 1,134 m ± 15 m and is uniform.
- **New region, no code.** Running the generator over a fixture pack for another region produces a valid manifest with no code change. Reuse `tests/features/api/fixtures/packs/ke-g6-east-africa.json` or a tiny synthetic one.
- **Within budget.** Every pack's assets meet the §3.6 budgets, and every manifest hash matches its file.

Unit tests to include:
- Terrain-RGB encode/decode round-trip.
- Tile maths (lon/lat ↔ XYZ, tile cover for bounds).
- River trace (cycles, dangling `NEXT_DOWN`, ending in a lake vs the ocean).
- Monotonic smoothing.
- The hypsometric ramp.
- Extent per feature kind.

Also produce **preview PNGs** (hillshade with the river overlaid) in `content-assets/<packId>/3d/previews/`, so a human can see the data without running the app.

- 🧑 **Checkpoint:** the founder looks at the previews. Is the Tana's course right? Do Mount Kenya, the Rift and the lakes look correct?

### Phase 2: the 3D viewer (mountains, lakes, valley)
- **Implemented 2026-11-17.** Lazy same-origin viewer, rise/flight, orbit/zoom, tappable labels,
  lake surfaces and static river ribbons; pack facts, exaggeration notes and licence notices.
  WebGL E2E checks rendering, rise, navigation, reduced motion, missing assets and 2D fallback.
  Measured: initial JS 148.8 kB gzip; lazy JS 252.3 kB gzip; first overview open 0.749 MB.
  Local software-rendered readiness (4 GB/medium tier): mountain 1.36 s; overview 1.13 s.
  Final feature/unit, typecheck, build, asset verification and all 30 E2E tests passed.
- **Implementation choices:** strided grids rather than RTIN; logarithmic depth avoids
  overview sea flicker; dedicated HTML portal keeps labels stable (ADR-0010).
  Frame-time probing, offline caching and label collision/terrain occlusion remain Phase 4.
  The founder still needs to review the look, touch controls and frame rate on a real phone,
  and confirm the exaggeration wording with a teacher. "Show river" is not built.
- **Entry point:** tapping a feature on the 2D map in explore mode, or the fact card's **"See in 3D"** button, opens the 3D view. A clear **"Back to map"** returns to 2D. The 3D code is a lazy chunk.
- The scene planner is pure and unit-tested. Rendering follows its plan.
- **E2E tests** (Playwright, WebGL2):
  - Tapping Mount Kenya opens a canvas that is not blank: the pixel variance passes a threshold, and the frame differs before and after the rise animation.
  - The height label shows the **pack fact** text.
  - **Back to map** works.
  - Every network request is same-origin.
  - With WebGL disabled (or low memory simulated), no 3D button appears and 2D still works.
  - With `prefers-reduced-motion`, there is no fly animation (the camera pose jumps to the end).
- **Bundle test:** the initial JS chunk does not grow, and the 3D chunk is lazy.

### Phase 3: Show river (the showpiece)
- A **Show river** action on river features. The water front sweeps from source to mouth. Expose its progress as a `data-flow-progress` attribute on the canvas container so tests can watch it. The camera follows, and the scene ends with the river mouth and the ocean in view.
- Water material: a scrolling flow normal map, with width that grows downstream, plus slight foam at the mouth.
- **E2E:**
  - `data-flow-progress` goes from 0 to 1 over time (Playwright clock).
  - The final camera position is within N km of the mouth.
  - Reduced motion shows the static river with a direction arrow.
- 🧑 **Checkpoint:** the founder watches it on their own phone with `npm run dev:phone`.

### Phase 4: polish to "wow", and offline
- Sky and atmospheric perspective (distance haze), ACES tone mapping, subtle bloom on `high` only.
- WorldCover-based albedo, baked AO, ocean shelf colour from bathymetry, a summit snow tint.
- Offline caching and an "About this map" panel with attributions.
- **Performance pass:** measure frame time per tier on the founder's phone (🧑) and record it in the technical guide. Tune the RTIN error threshold and texture sizes to meet ≥ 30 fps on `low`.
- **E2E:** after one visit, an offline reload (Playwright `context.setOffline(true)`) still renders the visited feature in 3D.

## 8. When to stop and ask (🧑)

- A licence is unclear, non-commercial, or needs a paid tier.
- A budget in §3.6 cannot be met without dropping a promised feature.
- A content question, such as border depiction, exaggeration wording, or a fact that disagrees with the data.
- Anything that would change the content pack schema in a way that is not backwards-compatible.
- Before committing more than 50 MB of LFS assets in total.

## 9. Out of scope

- AR or the camera. That is a separate track; the camera check already exists at `?check=camera`.
- AI-generated terrain or imagery. Geography must be accurate and reviewable. AI-generated decorative 3D props (animals, landmarks) may come later, with teacher review.
- 3D city buildings, weather simulation, and user-generated content.
- Any AI or LLM call at runtime for 3D. There is none; this is all deterministic rendering.
